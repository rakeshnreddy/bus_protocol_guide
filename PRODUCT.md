# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Design-verification engineers and hardware learners, ranging from protocol beginners to senior DV practitioners. They use the academy during focused study, waveform review, debug preparation, and day-to-day reference work, often for long sessions in changing ambient light.

## Product Purpose

Bus Protocol DV Academy is a local-first, visual learning environment for understanding and verifying AMBA bus protocols. It connects written protocol rules to signal timing, transaction ownership, stalls, ordering, and verifier behavior. Success means a learner can reason from a waveform or system diagram, identify what the protocol requires, and diagnose violations with confidence.

## Brand Personality

Technical, luminous, assured. The interface should feel like a carefully calibrated engineering instrument: precise enough for expert work, inviting enough for deliberate learning, and visually alive without competing with protocol content.

## Anti-references

- The current bare documentation-shell appearance with flat white surfaces and little hierarchy.
- Neon cyberpunk terminal themes that reduce readability or make every signal compete for attention.
- Generic SaaS dashboards with decorative metric cards unrelated to the learning task.
- Frosted-glass decoration on every container; glass belongs to navigation and structural overlays, while lesson content stays crisp.
- Motion that delays reading, loops continuously, or ignores reduced-motion preferences.

## Design Principles

1. Show protocol ownership before decoration: timing, direction, phase, and state remain visually dominant.
2. Make dense material navigable: learners should always know their protocol, lesson position, and next useful destination.
3. Use atmosphere to support focus: light and dark themes adapt to the learner's environment while preserving contrast.
4. Keep expert detail approachable: progressive disclosure and clear grouping reduce cognitive load without removing depth.
5. Preserve local-first trust: preferences remain on the device and the interface introduces no accounts, telemetry, or network dependency.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. All navigation, theme controls, visual interactions, and learning exercises must work by keyboard, expose meaningful semantics, meet comfortable touch-target sizing, retain visible focus, and avoid color-only meaning. Both themes must maintain readable contrast. Motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and dense diagrams must remain usable through contained horizontal scrolling at narrow widths and high zoom.
